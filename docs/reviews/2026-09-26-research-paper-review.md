"The Missing Rung" is a real question with a real mechanism, and the design is better than most I see at this stage. The within-design split is the smart part: graphic designers against web and interface designers, same field, different automatability. If their early-career paths diverge, you have evidence about the mechanism, not just a trend, and that comparison relies on your professional judgment in a way nobody else's paper could. The control group to rule out "a bad market for young people," the four ways you could be wrong written before the data, and the position you set out to test ("self-correction fails on *timing*, not on direction") make this a brief that can lose. That is what I look for first. The items below are in dependency order.

**Confirm the data exists before anything else.** You flagged it yourself: "CHECK FIRST: confirm all four occupations have early-career series." The entire mechanism test depends on it. If the dashboard gives you all four, the design holds. If it does not, you need a fallback before you commit the design. Public survey microdata split by age and occupation is one option, with the caveat that small occupations produce noisy age-group estimates. The other option is to narrow the comparison to the occupations that do have series. Settle this first, because it decides which control you can choose.

**Fill the brackets before you pull numbers.** The control occupation, the `[X]` gap for H1, `[YEAR]` for H2, and `[N]` years to mid-level are still open. You wrote the right rule ("Commit `[X]` and `[YEAR]` before pulling data or building the model"). Once the data question is settled, commit them. For the 3–5 year progression, stating it as your professional judgment is perfectly acceptable; label it that way.

**Plan how to see the price signal.** The self-correction argument depends on whether experienced wages are rising, and the standard occupational wage data report pay by percentile, not by experience. A high percentile (the 75th or 90th) can stand in for experienced pay if you say that is what it is. Decide which you will use.

**In order:**

1. Confirm which occupations have early-career series; choose the fallback if any do not.
2. Choose the control and commit `[X]`, `[YEAR]` and `[N]`.
3. Decide how experienced wages will be measured.

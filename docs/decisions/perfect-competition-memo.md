# Market Garden Bed Allocation — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Plant 10 tomato, 20 carrot, and 30 mesclun beds — 60
of the 64 available — and hire 4 temp workers.

## Why
Tomatoes stop at 10 beds because that's where they stop paying for
themselves: bed 10 costs $8,249 and sells for $8,800; bed 11 would cost
$9,391, more than it earns. Carrots and mesclun are planted to their full
caps (20 and 30) because their economics never says stop — both are
still $250–$400/bed under price when the caps end them. And labor was
never the limit: the plan uses about 5,277 of the 6,480 hours available
even with all 4 temp workers hired, so it's the caps holding carrot and
mesclun back, not hands.

## The judgment call
Both bed caps bind, but not equally: one more carrot bed is worth $352 in
profit, one more mesclun bed $246 — carrot ground is worth about 43% more
per bed. If I get the chance to rent or clear one more bed, carrot comes
first. I'm trusting that ranking for a bed or two past each cap; I
haven't tested whether it holds five or ten beds out, since returns past
a cap the model has never priced are a guess, not a result.

## What would change my answer
A tomato price drop of roughly 6% (below about $8,249/bed) would knock
tomato to 9 beds — the tightest margin in this plan. Carrot and mesclun
carry $250–$400/bed of headroom before their own economics would ever
ask to move, so a price swing there is far less dangerous. A fifth temp
worker wouldn't change anything either — only 3.16 of the 4 already
hired are actually needed.
